# 60-Minute Discovery Call Guide — Marcus Reyes, CEO, MedFlex

*Derived from D0D discovery questions. Use this during the call. Questions are ordered for the conversation — do not skip segments.*

---

## Before you start

**What you must leave this call knowing:**
1. Is coordinator bandwidth the actual bottleneck on fill rate — or is it candidate supply?
2. Does the credential gate get bypassed under time pressure, and is that tolerated?
3. What specifically caused the two prior AI failures?

If you don't get clear answers on those three, the architecture cannot be finalised.

**Marcus's profile:** Cuts off rambling. Respects challenge with substance. Has been burned by AI twice. Wants results in 8 weeks. Keep questions tight — one sentence each.

---

## 0–5 min — Context setting

*Goal: confirm how close Marcus is to the operational detail. If he's removed from day-to-day, flag that you'll need a follow-up session with a senior coordinator.*

- "How much of the shift-filling process do you see directly — are you in the operational detail, or do you see it through the numbers?"

---

## 5–15 min — Bottleneck (the core ROI question)

*Goal: validate or invalidate the central hypothesis. If the bottleneck is not coordinator bandwidth, the entire proposed scope must change.*

**Q3:** "When a shift goes unfilled, what's the most common reason — no available qualified nurse, nurses declining the offer, or your team running out of time to work it before the window closes?"

**Q4:** "Walk me through the last shift fill that went smoothly — right nurse, first contact, done. What did the coordinator actually do, and how long did it take?"

---

## 15–30 min — Data and matching reality

*Goal: find out what data exists in systems vs. what lives in people's heads. This determines the build pre-conditions.*

**Q12:** "When a coordinator wants to know if a nurse is available, do they look it up in a system — or do they call or text the nurse to find out?"

**Q2:** "When a coordinator checks what credentials a facility requires for a specific unit, where does that information live — in a system, a document, or their memory from working with that facility?"

**Q13:** "Are there things your coordinators regularly use to make matching decisions that aren't in any system — spreadsheets, email threads, personal notes?"

---

## 30–45 min — Credential gate and compliance

*Goal: establish whether the credential gate is a genuine hard stop or a soft one with a bypass culture. This determines the agent's autonomy at the most critical decision point.*

**Q1:** "When a coordinator verifies credentials before confirming a placement, are they pulling from an internal system, looking up state board websites manually, or using a third-party verification service?"

**Q11:** "When a nurse renews their licence, how quickly does that appear in your system — same day, next day, longer? And who updates it?"

**Q7:** "Has a placement ever gone forward without completed credential verification — time pressure, coordinator was confident about the nurse? What happens in that situation?"

**Q8:** "When a mismatch does reach a facility — the 7% case — who discovers it, what's the consequence for MedFlex, and who's accountable internally?"

---

## 45–55 min — Trust, risk tolerance, and prior failures

*Goal: understand what failed before, what Marcus will accept as autonomous agent action, and whether coordinators have a future role they can see.*

**Q14:** "The chatbot and the recommendation engine — what specifically went wrong with each? Output quality, how adoption was handled, or the use case itself?"

**Q16:** "If the agent confirms a placement that turns out to be a credential mismatch, what's the consequence for MedFlex and for your relationship with that hospital?"

**Q17:** "What would the minimum visible human oversight step need to look like — for you and for your coordinators — for you to feel comfortable letting the agent make placement decisions without reviewing every one?"

**Q15:** "If the agent handles 60–70% of straightforward fills autonomously, what do you see the 8 coordinators doing with that freed time — is that already mapped out, or still open?"

---

## 55–60 min — Close

*Goal: leave Marcus with three clear open questions so he knows the FDE is designing against real constraints, not pitching a generic AI solution.*

Summarise back:
> "Based on this conversation, three things will determine the architecture: whether the bottleneck is coordinator bandwidth or candidate supply, whether credential data is queryable in real time, and what oversight level you need to feel comfortable at launch. I'll confirm those in writing and come back with the architecture framing."

Ask: "Is there a senior coordinator I should spend 30 minutes with to walk through a real shift fill in detail?"

---

## Questions held in reserve

Use these if conversation opens space — do not force them if time is short.

- **Q5:** "When a nurse has most but not all required credentials — 7 of 8 — is there a rule for when to proceed, or does it depend on the coordinator?"
- **Q6:** "When credential verification is complete, where is that recorded — timestamped in a system, or not consistently logged?"
- **Q9:** "Think about the last three no-shows. What did those situations have in common — shift type, time of day, how the confirmation was handled?"
- **Q10:** "When a shift is at risk of going unfilled after several contacts, at what point does a coordinator escalate, and what does escalation look like?"
