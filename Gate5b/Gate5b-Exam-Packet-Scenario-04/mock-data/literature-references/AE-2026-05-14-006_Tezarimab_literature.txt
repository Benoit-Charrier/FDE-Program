[Vendor extracted abstract:]
We report three patients with relapsing multiple sclerosis who developed delayed-onset cytopenia 3-6 months after initiation of tezarimab, an anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody approved in 2023. Patient 1: a 47-year-old woman developed Grade 3 neutropenia (ANC 720/μL) 4 months after first infusion, requiring G-CSF support. Patient 2: a 52-year-old man developed Grade 4 thrombocytopenia (platelets 18,000/μL) 5 months after first infusion, requiring platelet transfusion and hospitalisation; his immune workup was consistent with immune thrombocytopenia. Patient 3: a 39-year-old woman developed combined neutropenia (ANC 920/μL) and thrombocytopenia (platelets 62,000/μL) 6 months after second infusion. None of the patients had alternative explanations after standard workup. We propose that delayed-onset cytopenia is an emerging signal for tezarimab and recommend ongoing vigilance. Two of the three patients (Patient 2 and Patient 3) were enrolled in independent clinical trials of tezarimab; Patient 1 was treated commercially.
